Keep endpoint exceptions temporary, owned, and reviewable.

Use this register when a customer or technician requests a deviation from an endpoint, access, patching, security, monitoring, or service policy. Record both sides of the decision, scope the compensating controls, assign authority, and make expiry and closure evidence explicit.

A five-stage exception lifecycle

1. Request

Give the exception a durable ID. Record the customer, affected assets, policy or control, requestor, business need, and the consequence of declining it.

2. Assess

Describe the risk created by approval, the blast radius, affected data and identities, alternative paths, and the compensating controls that can be tested.

3. Decide

Record the named authority, decision, scope, start date, conditions, and review or expiry date. A ticket or verbal request is not the approval record.

4. Monitor

Verify the exception remains inside its approved scope and that the compensating controls still operate. Reassess when the asset, owner, vendor, threat, or business need changes.

5. Close or renew

Remove the deviation, verify the standard control is restored, retain closure evidence, and update status. Renewal requires current evidence and a fresh decision.

Keep secrets elsewhere

Link to protected evidence rather than placing passwords, tokens, private keys, customer data, or sensitive logs directly in the shared register.

What the CSV records

The register uses fifteen fields: exception ID, client, affected scope, policy or control, requestor, business justification, risk if approved, risk if denied, compensating controls, approver, decision, start date, expiry or review date, closure evidence, and status.

Decision boundary: this worksheet does not approve an exception, establish legal compliance, or replace customer authorization. Use the responsible organization's policy, contract, risk authority, and technical controls.
Relationship disclosure: Nizlo, a Windows-first RMM and IT-management platform, maintains this vendor-neutral register. It can be used with any appropriate RMM, PSA, service desk, documentation system, or endpoint-management stack.
