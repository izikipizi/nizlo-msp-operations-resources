Turn a six-week MSP onboarding contract into a controlled handoff.

Six weeks is enough for one bounded pilot—not for discovering and rebuilding an entire MSP by assumption. Establish authority and access first, write SOPs from observed work, prove the operating model on one representative customer, then train and hand off with exceptions visible.

Week 1 · Bound the project

Confirm authority, exclusions, decision owners, access requests, a representative pilot customer, and measurable exit criteria.

Week 2 · Observe before prescribing

Shadow the real workflow, reconcile inventory and access, draft the SOP in parallel, and keep unknowns in a decision register.

Week 3 · Prove the design safely

Validate tenant mapping, endpoint lifecycle, alert-to-ticket routing, patch exceptions, recovery, remote support, and rollback on controlled systems.

Week 4 · Run one controlled pilot

Use go/no-go approval, waves, checkpoints, stop conditions, communications, and an exception log rather than a bulk rollout.

Week 5 · Reconcile and teach

Close coverage gaps, review audit and access quality, prove removal, publish owned documentation, and turn pilot evidence into NOC exercises.

Week 6 · Test the handoff

Have the NOC operate without the project lead, review evidence with decision owners, transfer recurring work, and publish residual risk before closeout.

Common scope failure: do not make “document everything” the first milestone. Capture the actual workflow while access and discovery proceed, then revise the SOP using pilot evidence and explicit exception paths.
Keep secrets out of the plan: link to the approved vault or system of record. Do not place passwords, recovery codes, private keys, tokens, backup contents, or regulated customer data in this CSV.
