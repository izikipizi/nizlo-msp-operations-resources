What the postmortem must prove

Scope and impact

Reconcile affected, unaffected, and unknown tenants, endpoints, identities, integrations, data, and business impact.

Timeline and decisions

Separate observed facts from inference, normalize timestamps to UTC, and preserve authority, options, and outcomes.

Cause and controls

Document direct and contributing conditions while retaining evidence of controls that worked—not only those that failed.

Action and closure

Give each corrective action an owner, due date, validation method, retest, closure approver, and remaining exception.

How to run the review

  1. Appoint a facilitator who was not the sole owner of the response decisions being reviewed.
  2. Freeze the working evidence index and timeline before the meeting; label facts, inferences, clock offsets, and unresolved gaps.
  3. Walk the incident from detection through recovery, recording why decisions were made with the evidence available at that time.
  4. Separate direct technical causes, contributing conditions, control performance, and accepted constraints.
  5. Close actions only after the named validation method and retest produce the required evidence.

This template is an operational review aid. It is not legal advice, forensic guidance, breach-notification advice, or a substitute for counsel, insurers, regulators, or qualified incident-response professionals.

Minimum closure rule

Service restoration does not close the review. The original scope must reconcile, material evidence gaps must remain visible, each corrective action must have an accountable owner, and every closed action must show independent validation or an explicitly approved exception.

RMM incident postmortem FAQ

What should an RMM incident postmortem include?

Include a reconciled scope, UTC timeline, confirmed impact, response decisions, evidence index, direct and contributing causes, controls that worked or failed, corrective actions, validation methods, retest dates, accepted risks, and closure evidence.

When should the postmortem be completed?

Begin evidence collection during the incident, then hold the review after immediate recovery while facts and decisions remain available. Track unfinished corrective actions separately through validation and closure.

How do you keep the review blameless but accountable?

Anchor conclusions to evidence, distinguish direct causes from contributing conditions, record successful controls as well as gaps, and assign corrective actions to accountable owners rather than attributing failure to individual intent.
