Daily: catch active failure

Review backup exceptions, security detections, stale endpoints, critical monitors, failed jobs, and alert-to-ticket routing while the evidence is still fresh.

Weekly: reconcile drift

Own patch and reboot failures, tune alert noise, find unmanaged devices, review capacity and risk, sample automation, and update documentation from completed change. Record stale, conflicting, or unknown devices in the endpoint inventory template.

Monthly: prove service quality

Test restores, review policy outcomes and privileged access, measure agent coverage, reconcile lifecycle and billing, test runbooks, and surface material exceptions.

Quarterly: challenge assumptions

Review backup configuration, exercise recovery, inventory persistent integrations and grants, and remove checks that have become ceremonial or unactionable.

RMM-and-backup operating example: review Nizlo's published RMM and cloud-backup suite, then assign recurring owners for endpoint coverage, alerts, patching, backup jobs, storage consumption, restore tests, exceptions, and client reporting.
IT service-provider operating boundary: a recurring checklist becomes a managed service only when every check has an agreed scope, cadence, evidence source, exception threshold, accountable owner, escalation path, and customer-facing outcome. Use the RMM for IT service providers guide to map those operating commitments to multi-customer monitoring, patch workflows, automation, alerting, remote support, and reviewable service evidence before rollout.
Adapt the cadence: customer contracts, system criticality, recovery objectives, regulation, operating hours, and approved risk decisions override a generic frequency. A quarterly check may need to be daily for one workload and unnecessary for another.
Keep evidence, not secrets: link to approved systems of record. Do not copy passwords, recovery keys, regulated data, private customer content, or privileged tokens into the CSV.
