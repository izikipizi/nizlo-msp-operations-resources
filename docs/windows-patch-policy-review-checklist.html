Vendor-neutral policy review

Review the policy before the patch cycle.

Use these 24 checks to turn a patching default into an approved operating policy. A reviewer should be able to identify what is covered, when devices move, who owns exceptions, how failed changes roll back, and what evidence closes each cycle.

1. Authority and scope

  1. Name the policy owner, approving authority, effective date, version, and next review date.
  2. Define included tenants, device classes, operating-system editions, locations, and supported ownership models.
  3. List exclusions explicitly and assign each exclusion an owner and compensating control.

2. Update classification

  1. Separate security, quality, driver, firmware, feature, and emergency updates.
  2. State which update sources and approval channels are authoritative.
  3. Define when an emergency change can bypass the standard cadence and who records the decision.

3. Rings and promotion

  1. Define representative lab, pilot, early-production, and broad-production rings.
  2. Set minimum observation time and measurable promotion criteria for every ring.
  3. Specify stop conditions for crashes, service degradation, application failure, or unexpected restart behavior.

4. Deadlines and restarts

  1. Set deployment and completion deadlines by update severity.
  2. Document maintenance windows, user notices, restart deferrals, and forced-restart limits.
  3. Define treatment for offline, sleeping, bandwidth-constrained, and rarely connected devices.

5. Exceptions

  1. Require a business reason, affected scope, risk statement, approver, and expiry date.
  2. Assign monitoring and compensating controls while the exception remains open.
  3. Require retest, disposition, and closure evidence so deferrals cannot become permanent by default.

6. Failure and rollback

  1. Define rollback triggers and the person authorized to stop or reverse a deployment.
  2. Document recovery prerequisites, backups, known-uninstall paths, and service-specific restoration steps.
  3. State how failed devices are isolated, triaged, reconciled, and returned to a supported state.

7. Evidence and reporting

  1. Record approval, targeting, timestamps, install results, restart state, and device-level exceptions.
  2. Reconcile discovered, targeted, installed, failed, excluded, offline, and unknown device counts.
  3. Define the cycle-close report, its audience, retention period, and link to unresolved work.

8. Testing and review

  1. Test policy behavior on representative devices before broad rollout.
  2. Review missed deadlines, false success, noisy exceptions, rollback use, and recurring failure patterns.
  3. Reapprove the policy after material tooling, client, regulatory, or operating-model changes.

Definition of review-ready

A patch policy is review-ready when another authorized operator can identify its scope, make the same promotion and exception decisions, stop an unsafe deployment, locate the required evidence, and prove that every endpoint has a known disposition. A green dashboard alone is not closure if failed, offline, excluded, or unknown devices are not reconciled.
