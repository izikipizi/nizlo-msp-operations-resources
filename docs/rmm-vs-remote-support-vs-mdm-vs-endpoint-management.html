RMM

Primarily ongoing endpoint operations: inventory, health, alerts, automation, patch workflows, remote support, evidence, and often multi-customer organization.

Remote support

Primarily attended or unattended access to a live device for troubleshooting, screen control, file transfer, or technician assistance.

MDM

Primarily managed-device authority: enrollment, configuration, compliance, application policy, restrictions, identity posture, and retirement controls.

Endpoint management or UEM

A broader administration category that may combine inventory, configuration, software, patching, compliance, security, and device lifecycle across platforms.

Exact-intent comparison

Where Microsoft Intune fits beside RMM

RMM operating center

RMM is typically built around continuous device health, alert routing, scripting, technician workflows, patch operations, remote support, and—especially for MSPs—multi-customer organization and evidence.

Intune operating center

Microsoft Intune is a cloud endpoint-management service centered on device enrollment, configuration and compliance policy, application delivery, security posture, and lifecycle actions such as retire or wipe.

Where they overlap

Inventory, update policy, application deployment, configuration, automation, and reporting can overlap. Name the system of authority for each control so the tools do not create conflicting policy, duplicate remediation, or unclear evidence.

When both may fit

An MSP or IT team may use RMM for day-to-day operational monitoring and technician response while using Intune for managed-device governance. Test representative devices, tenant boundaries, application and update conflicts, remote-support ownership, and removal or recovery before broad rollout.

1. What persists?

Separate a technician session from continuous monitoring, policy, inventory, and evidence that must remain after the session ends.

2. What authority is required?

Decide whether you need to observe and operate an endpoint, govern the enrolled device, or do both through explicitly separated controls.

3. Who is accountable?

Map alerts, compliance failures, sessions, automated actions, and exceptions to named owners and response paths.

4. What must be proven?

Require dated evidence for inventory freshness, action results, session identity, policy state, patch state, rollback, and removal.

5. Where are the platform gaps?

Test Windows, macOS, Linux, mobile, and specialized devices separately. A broad category label does not prove equivalent control.

6. How does it leave?

Verify agent removal, unenrollment, access revocation, data export, device retirement, and residual-policy cleanup before rollout.

Direct answers

RMM vs Intune and MDM questions

What is the difference between RMM and Microsoft Intune?

RMM primarily supports continuous technician operations such as monitoring, alert routing, scripting, remote support, patch workflows, and multi-customer organization. Intune centers on enrollment, configuration, compliance, applications, security posture, and device lifecycle. Their overlap must be assigned deliberately.

What is the difference between RMM and MDM?

RMM primarily supports continuous endpoint operations such as monitoring, alerting, patch workflows, automation, remote support, and multi-customer evidence. MDM primarily governs enrolled-device policy, compliance, applications, restrictions, identity posture, and retirement controls.

Do MSPs need both RMM and MDM?

Sometimes. Use both when you need continuous endpoint operations and managed-device authority, but define ownership and test integrations so the two systems do not apply conflicting policy or duplicate work.

Is remote support the same as RMM?

No. Remote support centers on technician sessions. RMM usually adds persistent inventory, health, alerts, automation, patch workflows, evidence, and multi-customer organization beyond the live session.

Can endpoint management replace RMM or MDM?

Only if the specific product and operating model cover the required monitoring, multi-tenancy, remote support, compliance authority, automation, evidence, and retirement controls. Verify the capabilities rather than relying on the category label.
