RMM vs MDM vs remote support vs endpoint management.

RMM primarily owns continuous endpoint operations; MDM primarily governs enrolled-device policy and compliance. Remote support centers on technician sessions, while endpoint management or UEM may combine several administration domains. Verify the exact product behavior instead of buying from the category name alone.

RMM

Primarily ongoing endpoint operations: inventory, health, alerts, automation, patch workflows, remote support, evidence, and often multi-customer organization.

Remote support

Primarily attended or unattended access to a live device for troubleshooting, screen control, file transfer, or technician assistance.

MDM

Primarily managed-device authority: enrollment, configuration, compliance, application policy, restrictions, identity posture, and retirement controls.

Endpoint management or UEM

A broader administration category that may combine inventory, configuration, software, patching, compliance, security, and device lifecycle across platforms.

1. What persists?

Separate a technician session from continuous monitoring, policy, inventory, and evidence that must remain after the session ends.

2. What authority is required?

Decide whether you need to observe and operate an endpoint, govern the enrolled device, or do both through explicitly separated controls.

3. Who is accountable?

Map alerts, compliance failures, sessions, automated actions, and exceptions to named owners and response paths.

4. What must be proven?

Require dated evidence for inventory freshness, action results, session identity, policy state, patch state, rollback, and removal.

5. Where are the platform gaps?

Test Windows, macOS, Linux, mobile, and specialized devices separately. A broad category label does not prove equivalent control.

6. How does it leave?

Verify agent removal, unenrollment, access revocation, data export, device retirement, and residual-policy cleanup before rollout.

Safe evaluation: choose authorized, non-critical devices and test one category requirement at a time. Record expected evidence and a stop condition before granting privileged access, applying policy, running automation, or enabling unattended control.
