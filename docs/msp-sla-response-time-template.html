Define what the SLA clock actually measures.

Separate acknowledgement, service restoration, and resolution; then document coverage, clock pauses, responsibilities, escalation, exclusions, evidence, and approval before a customer relies on the commitment.

Response is not restoration

An acknowledgement target measures when work is accepted. A restoration target measures when service returns or a workaround exists. A resolution objective concerns the final fix. Do not merge them into one ambiguous number.

Define the clock

State the supported time zone, intake channel, start event, business-hours calendar, permitted pause reasons, update cadence, and stop condition. Otherwise two parties can measure the same ticket differently.

Name both sides

Assign the provider owner and escalation role, then document customer duties for access, evidence, approvals, contacts, and timely decisions. A paused dependency should be visible, not silently omitted.

Review the denominator

Use authoritative ticket and incident timestamps, keep excluded work explicit, and review breached, paused, restored, unresolved, and unmeasurable records instead of reporting one unsupported percentage.

Safe use: the four priority rows are examples, not recommended response-time promises. How to apply it: workshop the matrix with service delivery, technical owners, on-call staff, account leadership, and the customer. Test the clock rules against representative incidents before incorporating the approved matrix into a managed services agreement or service schedule.
