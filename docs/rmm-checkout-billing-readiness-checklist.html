Make checkout the last gate, not the first surprise.

This vendor-neutral checklist reconciles pricing, trial timing, first-charge exposure, add-ons, payment authority, invoices, renewal, cancellation, and operational acceptance before an RMM evaluation becomes a paid subscription.

Reproduce the price

Name the billing unit, plan, quantities, add-ons, retained tools, taxes, and recurring total. Stop if the expected invoice cannot be independently recalculated.

Fix the trial clock

Record the exact expiry and first-charge date in the buyer's timezone. A relative “14 days” is not enough for an accountable purchasing decision.

Verify the offer

Capture the written event that earns any discount and whether availability is reserved. Do not treat an application or trial start as a paid-upgrade position.

Assign payment authority

Name who may approve the card, spending limit, billing contact, invoice archive, failed-payment escalation, and first-invoice review.

Understand the exit

Record renewal cadence, cancellation steps, notice requirements, credits, quantity changes, service impact, and the evidence needed to prove cancellation.

Require operational acceptance

Do not let checkout bypass unresolved security, monitoring, patching, automation, remote-access, removal, or evidence stop conditions.

Recommended sequence: run the controlled RMM pilot, close the go/no-go decision, then use this checklist immediately before checkout and again when the first invoice arrives.

RMM checkout questions

What should be checked before a trial converts to paid?

Record the billing unit, plan, trial end and first-charge dates, total cost, add-ons, discount trigger, payment authority, renewal and cancellation terms, invoice route, and the approved technical decision.

Does starting a trial reserve a promotional discount?

Do not assume it does. Record the vendor's written eligibility event and reservation rule before relying on a promotional position.

Who should approve the purchase?

Use named technical and business approvers with authority over operational risk, commercial terms, the payment method, and affected systems.

What happens after the first invoice?

A named owner should reconcile the plan, quantities, add-ons, discount, tax, and total to the approved checkout record and escalate discrepancies.
