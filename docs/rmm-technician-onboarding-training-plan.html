Train technicians on outcomes, not menu tours.

Use this vendor-neutral plan to test whether a technician can operate an RMM safely after onboarding. Each exercise requires observable evidence, a pass criterion, and a stop condition before independent access expands.

1. Secure access and role boundaries

Sign in with MFA, identify the assigned role, and show which sensitive actions require escalation. Passing means the technician can explain and respect the boundary.

2. Tenant, site, and endpoint identity

Locate a named endpoint without crossing customer boundaries, then reconcile hostname, operating system, owner, site, and last check-in.

3. Alert triage and routing

Interpret a controlled alert, validate device context and timestamps, suppress no evidence, and route the issue to a named owner with a reason.

4. Patch state and exceptions

Explain scan state, approval policy, reboot requirements, final outcome, and any exception without treating a dispatched job as proof of completion.

5. Safe automation execution

Review scope and prerequisites, run one harmless approved action on the intended endpoint, retain output, and demonstrate the rollback or stop path.

6. Remote-session authorization

Verify authorization, connect only to the intended device, avoid unattended access outside policy, and preserve the required audit trail.

7. Ticket and customer context

Move from a device or alert to the correct customer record and document the problem, action, evidence, owner, and next checkpoint clearly.

8. Reporting and evidence export

Produce a dated report or export whose filters, time range, source, and limitations are clear enough for another technician to reproduce.

9. First-endpoint troubleshooting

Diagnose a missing check-in by proving install, service state, identity, network path, tenant placement, and fresh telemetry in order.

10. Escalation and stop conditions

Recognize wrong-target risk, excessive privilege, unclear output, failed rollback, or cross-tenant ambiguity and stop before causing wider impact.

Completion rule: attendance is not evidence of readiness. Mark an exercise passed only when the named technician produces the required proof without unsafe prompting. Record remediation and repeat the same exercise after a failure.
Relationship disclosure: Nizlo, a Windows-first RMM and IT-management platform, maintains this plan. It is designed for training on any RMM and does not claim that Nizlo or another vendor requires the least training.
