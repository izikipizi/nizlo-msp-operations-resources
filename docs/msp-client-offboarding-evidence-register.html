Prove that an MSP offboarding is actually complete.

Coordinate customer handoff, access revocation, agent removal, data disposition, residual exceptions, acceptance, and post-exit verification in one vendor-neutral evidence register.

Download the CSVRead the full checklistCISA MSP guidance

Authorize the exit

Record the approved end date, decision owners, scope, constraints, successor, service cutoff, and communication path before changing access or agents.

Transfer with evidence

Reconcile endpoints, accounts, vendors, licenses, documentation, backups, incidents, and open work. Name the authorized recipient and acceptance owner for each handoff.

Revoke every access path

Disable interactive, service, API, emergency, delegated, VPN, vault, cloud, and vendor-portal access. Revoke tokens and sessions, then verify removal from both sides.

Verify after cutoff

Track failed agent removals and retained data as exceptions, obtain customer acceptance, and monitor for unexpected authentication, check-ins, jobs, alerts, or data flows.

Safe use: do not place passwords, secrets, recovery codes, private keys, tokens, backup contents, or regulated customer data in this register. Store sensitive transfers in an approved encrypted channel and record only the evidence reference.
Control basis: CISA’s MSP guidance tells customers to disable MSP accounts after the provider no longer manages infrastructure and recommends least privilege, account and infrastructure hygiene, logging, backup protection, and clear responsibility. NIST CSF 2.0 supports managed identities, revoked credentials, asset lifecycle control, and documented risk treatment. Adapt the register to contracts, law, insurance, and product-specific removal behavior.
Relationship disclosure: Nizlo, a Windows-first RMM and IT-management platform, maintains this template. It is designed for use with any MSP, RMM, remote-support, monitoring, backup, or security stack. It is an operating aid, not legal advice, an audit opinion, or proof that an offboarding is complete.
