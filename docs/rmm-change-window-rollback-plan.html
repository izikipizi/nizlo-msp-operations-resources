How to use it

  1. Copy the CSV into your spreadsheet, ticketing system, or change record.
  2. Replace generic owners and thresholds with named people and measurable values.
  3. Record UTC start and end times, evidence links, and an explicit decision owner before execution.
  4. Do not begin the next wave until the prior wave reconciles and the go/no-go owner records a decision.
  5. Keep failed, excluded, offline, and unknown endpoints open as owned exceptions after the window closes.

This is an operational planning aid, not a substitute for your organization’s change-control, security, backup, or incident-response requirements.

RMM change management questions

What should the template include?

Record scope, owners, planned times, approval authority, measurable success criteria, evidence, rollback triggers and actions, stakeholder communications, exception ownership, and closure status.

When should an RMM change be rolled back?

Roll back when a pre-agreed stop condition occurs—for example, incorrect targeting, loss of check-in, failed validation, unacceptable user impact, broken alert routing, or results that cannot be reconciled.

How large should the first rollout wave be?

Start with the smallest representative pilot that can prove identity, telemetry, alerting, an authorized action, and removal. Expand only after it reconciles and a named owner records a go decision.

Is this plan tied to Nizlo?

No. Nizlo maintains the resource, but the CSV is vendor-neutral and can be used for any RMM deployment, migration, policy change, or automation rollout.
