Use this pilot matrix on representative Macs before rollout. It separates continuous RMM operations from MDM governance, makes privacy and remote-support authority explicit, and turns failures into owned stop conditions rather than assumptions.

Prove enrollment and identity

Test Apple silicon and Intel where available. Keep serial, hardware UUID, user context, tenant, site, install evidence, agent health, and connectivity attributable across rename, sleep, and reconnect.

Separate RMM from MDM

Assign one system of authority—or an explicit coexistence rule—for profiles, FileVault, restrictions, applications, certificates, updates, scripts, remote support, and removal.

Bound permissions and actions

Document every privacy permission, shell privilege, automation target, consent rule, remote-support capability, and security-tool exception before the pilot expands.

Accept or stop with evidence

Require current inventory, owned alerts, observable actions, update-state reconciliation, session closure, audit export, clean removal, and time-bounded exceptions.

Worked example: inventory passes, remote support fails

A pilot Mac reports current inventory after sleep and reconnect. The same technician completes an attended session on the Windows pilot device, but the Mac session opens with a blank screen. Record the Mac inventory check as passed and the Mac remote-support check as failed. If attended support is required, hold the Mac rollout for that workflow.

Ask the Mac policy owner to verify the documented screen capture permission for the exact support component under the standard user's policy. Record the policy owner and retest date in the matrix. To clear the gate, demonstrate that the technician can see the screen after approved consent, that access ends when the session closes, and that the session is attributable in the audit record.

Test what happens when a Mac user declines remote support

On one authorized pilot Mac with attended support required, have the test user decline the first request. Confirm the technician sees that it was declined and cannot view or control the device. Check whether the audit record distinguishes the declined request from a started session.

Start a separate request, approve it under the same policy, and verify that viewing and control end when the session closes. Record both outcomes under MAC-16 and the session closure under MAC-17 in the CSV. Hold rollout if a declined request permits access. Review Apple's privacy permission documentation for the Mac policy and the vendor's documentation for its separate session approval.

Start the pilot with one Mac

  1. Choose one authorized, noncritical Mac. Record its architecture, expected customer and site, and the MDM that owns configuration profiles and updates.
  2. Install the matching package. Verify the device appears in the expected site, then refresh inventory and confirm that the console shows a new check-in. A device in the wrong site or a stale check-in needs investigation before the pilot expands.
  3. Record remote support separately. Use the normal technician role and test both declining and approving an attended request. Confirm access ends when the approved session closes.

Enter the check-in time, evidence link, owner, and retest date in the pilot CSV. Use the full matrix before expanding beyond the test device.

Before enrolling another Mac, check what happens after sleep and reconnect, especially if your users work away from the office.

Verify that the same Mac checks in after sleep

  1. Record the pilot Mac's device identifier, customer and site, and last check-in. Agree on an acceptable offline threshold for your team.
  2. Let the authorized test Mac sleep beyond that threshold. Check whether the console shows the device's last contact and distinguishes stale inventory from a fresh report.
  3. Wake it and reconnect normally. Confirm that the same record shows a new check-in. If a second record appears or inventory stays stale, capture the two timestamps and leave rollout on hold until a retest passes.

Record the result under MAC-22 in the pilot CSV. If your Macs use FileVault, run the restart test separately. If Nizlo is on your shortlist, review its macOS coverage and pilot setup.

Check when a FileVault Mac returns after restart

Use one approved, noncritical Mac with your existing FileVault and MDM policies. Confirm who handles unlock and recovery, and agree how long a device can be offline during maintenance. Keep any recovery key in the approved system.

  1. Before the restart, record the macOS build, device record, last RMM check-in, and the FileVault status reported by the system responsible for encryption.
  2. During the approved restart, record what the console shows before an authorized user unlocks the Mac. After unlock, time the first fresh check-in and confirm it belongs to the same device record.
  3. Test the remote support workflow separately after sign-in using the normal consent policy. Record when it becomes usable. Compare these timings with the team's maintenance window.

Record encryption ownership under MAC-19, reconnect under MAC-22, and remote support under MAC-16 in the CSV. If the required workflow depends on someone being onsite to unlock a remote Mac, document who can do that before expanding the rollout. See Apple's FileVault device management guidance for the encryption and recovery responsibilities.

Run the matrix on a mixed fleet

Start with one authorized, noncritical Mac and one representative Windows device. Record the tenant and site, verify that both devices check in, and capture one inventory result and one support result per operating system. Keep your existing MDM in charge of Mac configuration and updates until you have tested who owns each control.
