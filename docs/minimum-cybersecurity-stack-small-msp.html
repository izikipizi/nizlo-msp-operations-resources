Vendor-neutral baseline · Small-business clients

Build a minimum cybersecurity stack around outcomes, not logos.

Use this baseline to decide which controls every supported client needs, what evidence proves each control is working, and who owns exceptions. Adjust depth for the client's data, exposure, contracts, insurance, and legal or regulatory obligations.

1. Identity and privileged access

Require MFA, separate administrative accounts, least privilege, conditional access where appropriate, emergency access governance, and a documented joiner-mover-leaver process.

2. Endpoint operations and detection

Maintain trustworthy inventory, patch visibility, secure configuration, endpoint detection and response, controlled remote support, application oversight, and owned alert routing.

3. Email and cloud protection

Use anti-phishing controls, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, risky sign-in review, mailbox forwarding review, application-consent governance, and retained cloud audit evidence.

4. Backup and tested recovery

Protect required endpoint, server, SaaS, and configuration data with isolated recovery paths. Monitor job outcomes, retain evidence, and prove restores on a schedule.

5. Network, DNS, and web controls

Operate supported firewalls, restrict management exposure, separate sensitive networks where needed, and use secure DNS or web filtering when it reduces a defined risk.

6. Vulnerability and external exposure

Identify assets and internet-facing services, prioritize by exploitation and business context, remediate within owned targets, and time-bound every accepted exception.

7. Access lifecycle and vendor control

Inventory privileged integrations, remote-access paths, service accounts, and supplier access. Review them regularly and revoke them promptly during offboarding.

8. Logging, incident response, and evidence

Retain useful identity, endpoint, email, firewall, and administrative evidence; define escalation and communication ownership; and exercise the response path.

Important boundary: an RMM can support inventory, monitoring, patch visibility, automation, evidence collection, and controlled remote operations. It is not a substitute for EDR or MDR, identity protection, email security, tested backup, or an incident-response process.
Prove coverage: use one row per endpoint and control plane in the stack-alignment register. Record expected state, observed state, freshness, evidence, and an exception owner. An installed agent or console record alone does not prove that protection is current and correctly assigned.
Control cost without hollowing out the baseline: compare technician time, alert noise, false positives, integration work, training, recovery testing, and exception handling alongside license price. Consolidate only when a bounded pilot proves the replacement produces the required evidence and operational outcome.
Primary-source foundation: use the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 Small Business Quick-Start Guide to align these operational controls with a risk-management program. CISA's hardening guidance for MSPs and small and mid-sized businesses reinforces MFA, least privilege, protected backups, and deliberate trust boundaries between providers and customers. These sources inform the baseline; they do not certify this checklist or any product.
