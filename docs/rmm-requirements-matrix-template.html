What belongs in a usable requirement

Business reason

State the operational or risk outcome behind the requirement. A feature name alone does not explain why the capability matters or what tradeoff is acceptable.

Evidence source

Ask for dated documentation, contract language, an export, or observed pilot evidence. A yes/no sales response is not proof.

Pilot test and acceptance

Describe the controlled action and observable result that will pass the requirement. Keep production credentials and customer data out of test records.

Owned gaps

Record partial support, dependencies, compensating controls, remediation commitments, owners, and target dates instead of averaging a failed critical control into a score.

A practical RMM selection workflow

Replace the examples with your environment

Start with client count, endpoint classes, technician roles, service hours, regulatory obligations, current tooling, network constraints, and exit requirements.

Set priority before vendor review

Use Must, Should, Could, and Will not evaluate—or your approved equivalent—before demos begin. Do not silently convert an unmet Must into a low weighted score.

Request evidence, not adjectives

Map each response to a source: official documentation, security material, contractual language, dated quote, export, or controlled pilot observation.

Test representative workflows

Use authorized lab or pilot endpoints across the operating systems, proxies, permissions, patch states, alert routes, and remote-access policies you actually expect to manage.

Separate product gaps from implementation work

Record whether a miss requires configuration, integration, process change, custom development, a roadmap promise, or an accepted exception. Assign an owner and date.

Reconcile requirements, pilot evidence, and price

Use the requirements matrix to define fit, the evaluation scorecard to record observed results, and the quote worksheet to compare full commercial terms.

Coverage included in the starter matrix

Control and architecture

Ownership, tenant or site scope, audit records, data boundaries, agent communication, proxy behavior, and outage resilience.

Identity and privileged actions

MFA, least-privilege roles, session revocation, script targeting, staged automation, remote-access policy, and per-action evidence.

Monitoring and endpoint operations

Thresholds, recovery, routing, correlation, service timing, patch rings, exceptions, inventory identity, reporting, and lifecycle cleanup.

Security, implementation, and exit

Vendor response paths, evidence preservation, agent removal, implementation ownership, commercial terms, support, export, and transition readiness.

RMM requirements matrix questions

What is an RMM requirements matrix?

A structured worksheet that maps each operational, security, technical, commercial, and exit requirement to its priority, business reason, vendor response, evidence, pilot test, acceptance criteria, gaps, owner, and decision.

How do you use it in an RMM RFP?

Replace the examples with your environment, set priorities before demos, require every vendor to answer the same rows, request evidence for material claims, and carry unresolved gaps into pilot testing and contract review.

Which requirements should be mandatory?

That depends on your customers, endpoint mix, technician roles, service hours, security obligations, integrations, and exit plan. Treat the examples as prompts and have accountable owners approve the final Must requirements.

Should responses be scored before a pilot?

A response can support screening, but important capabilities should be verified with dated documentation, contract language, exports, or controlled pilot evidence. Do not let a weighted average hide a failed critical requirement.

Is the CSV free to download?

Yes. The matrix is public and ungated. No account or email address is required, and the examples can be edited for any RMM evaluation.
