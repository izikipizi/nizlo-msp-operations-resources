Review who can do what in your RMM.

Map each technician or role to its customer scope, device scope, privileged capabilities, business justification, approvals, authentication controls, review dates, and remediation owner.

Download the CSVNIST least privilegeCISA MSP guidance

Start with scope

Record the customer, tenant, site, and device boundary for every assignment. A narrow capability across every customer can still be excessive privilege.

Separate capabilities

Review remote control, background tools, scripts, automation, patch approval, alert changes, credential access, role administration, APIs, and commercial data independently.

Require evidence

Name the business reason, approver, approval time, last review, next review, and any temporary expiration rather than treating a role name as proof.

Close exceptions

Record retain, reduce, revoke, or investigate as a decision, then assign every open exception a ticket and remediation owner.

Safe use: the included row is an example. Delete it before use. Do not record passwords, recovery codes, access tokens, API secrets, or vault contents in the matrix.
How to apply it: review high-impact capabilities and broad customer scope first; verify MFA and managed-admin-device requirements; remove access immediately when employment or duties change; and give temporary elevation an explicit expiration. The template is an operating aid, not a certification or substitute for product-specific access testing.
Relationship disclosure: Nizlo, a Windows-first RMM and IT-management platform, maintains this template. It is designed for use with any RMM or privileged-access workflow and does not claim that every product exposes every listed permission.
