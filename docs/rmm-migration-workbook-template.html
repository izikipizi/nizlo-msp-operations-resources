Example RMM migration project plan

Use these six stages as a sequencing model, not a universal duration. Compress or extend the schedule around estate size, customer change windows, integrations, offline endpoints, and the time required to observe ordinary and failure states.

  1. Stage 0Confirm scope, authority, success measures, source-access dates, communication owners, and rollback boundaries.
  2. Stage 1Export and reconcile endpoints, customers, sites, policies, scripts, integrations, credentials, and commercial dependencies.
  3. Stage 2Build destination structure, roles, monitoring, patching, alert routes, automation, reporting, and evidence locations.
  4. Stage 3Run a representative pilot, test failure and rollback, explain defects, retest, and record the go, revise, or stop decision.
  5. Stage 4Move endpoints through owned waves while one system at a time controls patches, scripts, reboots, alerts, and remote access.
  6. Stage 5Reconcile every endpoint and exception, remove source access, preserve required records, close licenses, and obtain acceptance.

RMM migration checklist questions

Use these answers to turn a tool change into a controlled operational project. The workbook supplies the fields for owners, evidence, dependencies, dates, rollback, and decision status.

What belongs in an RMM migration checklist?

Cover source inventory, customer and site mappings, access roles, policy ownership, pilot devices, coexistence, deployment waves, rollback, reconciliation, source-agent removal, credential revocation, evidence retention, and acceptance.

How long should two RMM tools overlap?

Use the shortest controlled overlap that preserves recovery. Assign monitoring, patching, scripts, alerts, reboots, and remote access to one system at a time, then give the overlap an owner and end date.

Which endpoints should move first?

Begin with authorized, representative, noncritical endpoints. Include common hardware, operating-system builds, network conditions, and business applications while keeping fragile servers and high-impact systems for later waves.

How do you prove the migration is complete?

Reconcile every source endpoint, verify destination workflows, explain each exception, remove old agents and access paths, revoke obsolete credentials, retain required evidence, and record acceptance.

How do you turn the checklist into a project plan?

Sequence the controls into owned stages, then give each stage dependencies, evidence requirements, decision dates, and stop conditions. Adjust the timing to your estate and change windows instead of treating a sample schedule as universal.

Turn the checklist into an owned project plan

Filter the CSV by phase, add the accountable owner and decision date, link approved evidence, and record dependencies and rollback before a wave starts. A stage is complete only when its expected result is observable and its exceptions have owners.
