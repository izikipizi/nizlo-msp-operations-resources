Plan the boundary

Name authorized devices, technicians, customers, dates, prohibited actions, measurable pass criteria, stop conditions, and independent recovery paths.

Prove identity and enrollment

Test unique technician and device identities, MFA, revocation, role scope, installer handling, and deterministic tenant placement.

Exercise daily operations

Follow alerts into tickets, compare inventory and patch state, control restarts, preview automation targets, and preserve failure evidence.

Challenge privileged paths

Test consent, remote-session termination, cross-tenant object access, audit reconstruction, exports, and retention rather than assuming the happy path.

Test failure and recovery

Make offline state, stale data, queued work, cancellation, service interruption, bounded retries, and reconciliation visible before production.

Remove and decide

Prove clean uninstall and credential revocation, record residual risk, then choose go, limited go, remediate and retest, or no-go.

RMM proof of concept questions

What should an RMM proof of concept test?

Test authority, technician and endpoint identity, enrollment, tenant placement, monitoring freshness, patch-state reconciliation, automation targeting, remote-access controls, audit evidence, failure recovery, clean removal, and the final rollout decision.

How long should an RMM pilot run?

Define the dates before installation and allow time for setup, ordinary operations, a patch or maintenance event, failure tests, retesting, removal, and a scheduled decision review. A fixed window prevents a pilot from becoming an unowned production deployment.

How many endpoints belong in an RMM trial?

Start with a small, authorized sample that represents the operating systems, sites, network conditions, roles, and policies expected in production. Expand only after the first group passes the documented acceptance gates.

What evidence should an RMM pilot keep?

Keep timestamps, endpoint and technician identifiers, configuration exports, screenshots or logs, expected and observed results, defects, remediation, retest dates, approvers, residual risks, and the final go, limited-go, retest, or no-go decision.

Use one evidence chain from installation to rollout: troubleshoot the first endpoint, apply the first-endpoint acceptance gates, and reconcile the result with the patch-compliance calculator before expanding the pilot.
Use authorized lab or noncritical systems: do not create destructive failures, cross customer boundaries, weaken security controls, or include production-critical targets without written authority and tested recovery.
Evidence is part of the result: a pass without a reproducible observation, export, timestamp, or approval record is not decision-quality evidence. Keep secrets and regulated customer data in their approved systems of record.
