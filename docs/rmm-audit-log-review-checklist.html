RMM audit log review questions

What should an RMM audit log contain?

An RMM audit log should identify the actor, customer or tenant, endpoint or target, timestamp and timezone, action, relevant parameters, authorization context, outcome, and a durable event identifier. Test console, API, agent, automation, patching, scripting, remote-session, identity, credential, integration, and configuration activity rather than relying on sign-in records alone.

How often should an MSP review RMM audit logs?

Monitor or alert on high-risk events continuously when practical. Add a documented recurring review at a cadence based on risk, contracts, and obligations, and review evidence after privileged changes, exceptions, incidents, and material platform updates. The checklist records both cadence and owner so gaps have a clear follow-up path.

How should RMM audit evidence be retained?

Keep authorized references in the checklist while storing sensitive exports, credentials, recordings, restricted reports, and customer data in approved evidence storage. Set retention periods from contracts, incident-response needs, legal obligations, and risk, then verify that exports preserve timestamps, identifiers, attribution, completeness, and integrity.

How to use it: run harmless approved test actions in a bounded tenant and endpoint scope, then replace each “Not reviewed” status with evidence-backed results. Keep credentials, session recordings, secrets, restricted assurance reports, and customer-sensitive exports in approved evidence storage; put only authorized references in the CSV. Use the RMM vs EDR comparison to separate operational evidence from threat-detection and response evidence.
Why these controls matter: joint cybersecurity guidance for MSPs recommends logging provider delivery activity, protecting important logs, and giving customers agreed visibility. CISA also advises keeping direct access to log storage outside the reach of RMM tools. Use the joint MSP security advisory and CISA remote-access guidance as primary-source context, then set retention and review requirements for your contracts, risks, and obligations.
