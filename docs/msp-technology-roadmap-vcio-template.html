Require current-state proof

Link inventories, lifecycle dates, service records, security evidence, restore tests, contracts, and budgets. Mark unknowns as unknown instead of treating absence of evidence as a healthy state.

Sequence by dependency

Order initiatives by urgency, prerequisite work, operating capacity, maintenance windows, customer readiness, and funding—not by whichever proposal was written first.

Close every decision

Record whether each item is approved, rejected, deferred, accepted as risk, or waiting for information. Give it an owner, next date, success measure, and evidence link.

Safe use:

This register is an operational planning aid, not legal, financial, compliance, security, or procurement advice. Replace example questions with the customer's agreements, risk process, authoritative records, approved budget, and decision rights.

Commercial boundary:

A roadmap should not turn every finding into a sale. Preserve no-action, risk-acceptance, scope-change, and evidence-needed decisions, and keep recurring service obligations separate from optional project work.

MSP technology roadmap questions

What belongs in the roadmap?

Use agreed goals, scope, current-state evidence, target states, risks, initiatives, dependencies, cost, owners, decisions, success measures, and review dates.

How is this different from a QBR?

The QBR reviews a period. The roadmap carries decisions and initiatives across the planning horizon. Use the QBR to reconcile and update it.

Should every issue become a project?

No. Record remediation, operating change, project, risk acceptance, scope change, further evidence, or no action as distinct outcomes.

Is it tied to one platform?

No. Use the systems and approved records that actually hold the evidence.
