How to use it

  1. Import the CSV into a spreadsheet, ticket, or change-management record.
  2. Replace generic owners, dates, and rollback triggers with named people and measurable thresholds.
  3. Complete the pilot controls before approving a wider wave.
  4. Reconcile successful, failed, excluded, offline, and unknown endpoints after every wave.
  5. Keep exceptions open until each has an owner, due date, evidence, and disposition.

The checklist is an operational planning aid. It does not replace your organization’s security, backup, change-control, or incident-response requirements.

RMM implementation checklist questions

What should an RMM implementation checklist include?Define ownership and scope, reconcile the endpoint inventory, verify prerequisites, test representative pilot devices, set deployment-wave and rollback thresholds, reconcile every outcome, and retain acceptance evidence.
Is implementation different from deployment?Implementation is the full operating change: goals, inventory, policy design, pilot, technician readiness, deployment, acceptance, and stabilization. Deployment is the narrower act of installing and enrolling agents, so a complete implementation plan must cover both.
How should an MSP deploy RMM agents?Begin with authorized noncritical pilot endpoints. Verify identity, telemetry, alerting, patch policy, automation, remote-support authority, and removal before expanding in bounded waves.
How do you verify an RMM rollout is complete?Account for successful, failed, excluded, offline, and unknown endpoints; verify check-in and policy state; assign every exception; and record explicit operational acceptance.
What stops an RMM deployment wave?Pause when install or check-in failures cross the approved threshold, endpoint identity is ambiguous, critical workflows fail, security controls conflict, or rollback cannot be completed safely.
