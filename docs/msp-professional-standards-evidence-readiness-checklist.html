MSP professional standards readiness starts with evidence.

Industry groups are developing more consistent expectations for IT service providers. This worksheet helps an MSP organize the evidence it already has, expose gaps, assign owners, and retest improvements—without pretending a self-assessment is a certification.

Important: this is not a GTIA, Texas A&M, CRITS, regulatory, audit, or certification instrument. It does not predict the final content of any developing standard. Use authoritative program materials for formal requirements and independent assessors for assurance.

Governance and workforce

Name accountable leadership, policy owners, obligations, privileged roles, competency expectations, and recurring review.

Customer and service evidence

Keep scope, shared responsibilities, asset coverage, service clocks, escalation, performance measures, and exceptions reviewable.

Security and operational control

Test privileged access, monitoring, patching, remote actions, logs, incidents, change, backups, and continuity with bounded evidence.

Commercial and lifecycle control

Review suppliers, privacy, pricing and renewal terms, customer assurance, exit readiness, corrective action, and retesting.

Use the register as a gap-closing loop

Choose one domain, link each control to current evidence, mark absent or ambiguous proof as a gap, name the remediation owner and due date, then retest the same control. Do not mark a control complete because a policy exists; confirm that the operating evidence shows the policy is used.

What this checklist does—and does not—do

GTIA says its Consortium for Responsible IT Services is working toward a self-regulatory framework for the profession. This public worksheet responds to the practical evidence-readiness problem; it does not restate draft requirements, grant membership, certify conformity, or replace the eventual official materials.

Primary context: read GTIA's Consortium announcement and its public MSP Guidebook. If the final framework conflicts with this worksheet, the official framework controls.
Keep secrets out of the register: link to approved systems of record. Do not place passwords, tokens, private keys, recovery codes, regulated customer data, raw backup contents, or other secrets in the CSV.
